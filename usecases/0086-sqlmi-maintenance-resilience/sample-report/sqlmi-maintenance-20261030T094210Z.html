What Azure allows, what it does not and what to do

The platform limits, and the order of the measures that do depend on you.

What Azure allows

  • 3 windows: default (17:00–08:00), weekday (Mon–Thu 22:00–06:00) and weekend (Fri–Sun 22:00–06:00), in the local time of the region.
  • Notice 24 h before, at the start and at the end through Service Health alerts.
  • Different windows on the primary and the secondary of a failover group.
  • Redirect connection type (fewer disconnections from gateway maintenance).
  • Manual failover for drills (max. 1 every 15 min).

What Azure does not allow

  • Choosing an exact day and hour, or custom windows as on-premises.
  • Postponing or canceling a specific maintenance.
  • Avoiding unplanned events (hardware, network) or critical updates that can be applied outside the window.
  • A reconfiguration without disconnection: it always drops connections for a few seconds.

What to do (in this order)

  1. Retries with backoff in every application: they remove the impact of planned and unplanned events.
  2. Maintenance alert + communication to the business.
  3. Weekday or weekend window based on the real load; different windows in the failover group.
  4. Redirect instead of Proxy.
  5. Quarterly drill in pre-production with a manual failover.
